# Progressive Capital Gains Companion Proposal

## What works well at small scale can break at large scale

---

## Preamble: Purpose of the Framework

This document presents a plain-language framework for understanding how preferential tax treatment of capital income behaves as annual realized capital income grows very large. It is not legislation. It is not a proposal for immediate policy adoption. It does not prescribe implementation details. Its purpose is to explain a structural issue, isolate it from adjacent debates, and outline a narrow way of thinking about it that avoids more disruptive alternatives.

---

## Why this framework exists

### 1. What works well at small scale can break at large scale

The current system for taxing long-term capital gains and qualified dividends works reasonably well for most investors. Gains are often irregular, tied to long-term saving, and reflective of risk, inflation, and patience. Preferential rates help encourage investment, capital formation, and long-horizon decision-making. Problems emerge when the same structure is applied to very large and recurring amounts of capital income. At that point, the system shapes how large pools of capital compound over time rather than marginal decisions. Rules designed for ordinary investment activity begin producing system-level effects when applied at scale.

### 2. The issue is not the rate, but the ceiling

Public debate often focuses on whether capital gains rates should be higher or lower. The most important feature is the permanent ceiling on those rates. Once the top preferential rate ceiling is reached, the same marginal rate applies to a $50,000 gain above the ceiling and a $50 million gain above the ceiling — despite the vastly different economic implications. At that point, the tax system stops scaling with economic impact. Flat ceilings allow large capital income to compound under rules designed for much smaller activity.

### 3. Capital income at scale is usually reinvested, not consumed

Large capital gains and dividend flows are rarely used for day-to-day spending. They are more often reinvested, used as collateral, or deployed to acquire additional assets. Their dominant effect is accelerating future capital growth. This framework focuses on compounding dynamics rather than consumption behavior. It deliberately avoids labor-income analogies and lifestyle-based comparisons.

### 4. Why this analysis stays inside the realization system

Wealth taxes and mark-to-market systems introduce valuation problems, liquidity risks, and avoidance incentives. They require ongoing asset valuation and taxation without sale. This framework deliberately avoids those approaches. It asks whether preferential rate ceilings should remain flat as realized capital income grows by orders of magnitude. All scaling occurs only at realization.

---

## Why this framework reduces pressure for wealth taxes

- Wealth taxes gain support when people believe large concentrations of capital cannot be meaningfully reached under the existing system.
- That pressure is driven by perceived structural failure rather than ideology.
- Scale-sensitive rate ceilings operate within the realization framework.
- They introduce increasing friction at extreme scale without asset-based taxation.
- This reduces political demand for wealth taxes.

## Mechanics & Implications

### 1. Progressive rate ceilings change outcomes gradually, not abruptly

The framework does not introduce sharp cliffs. It does not impose sudden penalties. Effective rates rise gradually as annual realized capital income grows. There is no single threshold where effects suddenly become severe.

### 2. The framework targets compounding capacity, not ordinary investment decisions

- Most investment decisions are driven by expected returns, risk, liquidity needs, and time horizon.
- They are not driven by small differences in tax rates.
- The framework does not influence asset selection or portfolio construction.
- It affects how very large amounts of realized capital income compound over time.

### 3. Early progressivity is intentionally gentle

- Initial progressive ranges are deliberately modest.
- The framework is not attempting to equalize outcomes.
- It is not attempting to correct ordinary behavior.
- It establishes that preferential treatment is not infinitely scalable.

### 4. Large one-time realizations are affected — explicitly

- Because scaling is annual, a single large realization can enter higher ranges.
- This is not an unintended consequence.
- It follows directly from using realized magnitude as the scaling variable.
- Both recurring and one-time realizations are affected when they occur.
- Strong convergence occurs only at very high realization levels.

### 5. The framework changes end states, not day-to-day decisions

- The framework does not force sales.
- It does not require asset valuation.
- It does not alter how individual investments are chosen.
- It reshapes long-run trajectories rather than short-run behavior.

## Behavioral Effects

### 1. What the framework discourages

- Reliance on permanently capped preferential rates at extreme scale as a long-term strategy.
- Structuring activity primarily around harvesting very large gains indefinitely under a flat ceiling.

### 2. What the framework does not discourage

- Long-term investing.
- Buy-and-hold strategies.
- Dividend-paying assets.
- Portfolio rebalancing.
- Ordinary liquidity-driven sales.

### 3. Effects on timing and realization behavior

Because scale is measured annually, realization timing matters at the margin. The absence of sharp cliffs limits incentives for extreme timing manipulation. There is no single threshold that must be avoided at all costs.

### 4. Effects on one-time and episodic events

The framework does not attempt to exempt them categorically. Defining one-time events invites complexity and scope creep. The framework favors simple, transparent, scale-based rules.

### 5. Summary of behavioral intent

- Discourage structural reliance on flat preferential ceilings at extreme scale.
- Preserve ordinary investment behavior.
- Minimize artificial timing strategies.

---

## Limitations & Non-Goals

- This framework does not attempt to solve deferral or timing of realization. It acknowledges that deferral pricing elsewhere strengthens its effectiveness, but deferral itself remains outside its scope.
- It does not address borrowing against unrealized gains or step-up mechanics. These are treated as separate structural problems with separate tools.
- It is not a wealth tax and does not tax net worth or unrealized appreciation.
- It does not aim for universal parity between capital and labor income.
- It does not distinguish intent, motivation, or frequency of realizations.
- It does not eliminate large fortunes or cap wealth.
- It is not designed to maximize revenue.
- It is analytical, not self-executing or legislative.
- These limitations are deliberate design choices.

## Relationship to Other Tools (Including TWDC)

### 1. This framework and TWDC address different structural dimensions

- This framework addresses **scale**: how preferential tax treatment behaves as annual realized capital income grows very large.
- The Time-Weighted Deferral Cost (TWDC) framework addresses **time**: how long appreciation remains unrealized while retaining preferential treatment.
- These are distinct problems.
- Neither framework attempts to solve the other's domain.

### 2. Progressive rate ceilings do not solve deferral

Even with scale-sensitive rate ceilings, appreciation can remain unrealized for very long periods. This framework does not penalize holding. It does not price time. Those behaviors are intentionally outside its scope.

### 3. TWDC does not solve scale

TWDC applies cost based on duration of deferral, not magnitude of realization. Large realizations that occur quickly can still benefit from flat preferential ceilings without a scale-based adjustment. TWDC alone does not address extreme annual realization size or repeated large realizations.

### 4. Why deferral pricing materially strengthens scale-sensitivity

Raising the effective rate at realization increases the relative value of not realizing. As the top rate under this framework rises, the option to hold an appreciated asset, borrow against it, and defer sale indefinitely becomes more attractive at the margin. Left unpriced, that option operates against the framework's own objective: it reduces the likelihood that large realizations occur at all, and therefore reduces the framework's practical reach.

TWDC prices that option. The Deferral Cost Charge applies annually to unrealized appreciation, and the Time-Weighted multiplier increases with duration, so the cost of holding is no longer zero. Borrowing neutrality provisions further ensure that converting unrealized appreciation into liquidity triggers a realization-equivalent event. Deferral remains available, but it is no longer costless.

This is not a claim that this framework is inoperative without TWDC. It is a claim about behavioral interaction: the framework's intended effect — raising the cost of large realizations — depends on deferral not being a costless alternative. TWDC ensures that condition holds. The two frameworks remain administratively independent and can be evaluated separately. The framework's effectiveness is materially stronger when deferral is priced.

### 5. Together, the frameworks close complementary gaps

When considered together, scale is no longer free. Time is no longer free. Preferential treatment remains, but it is no longer unlimited along either dimension. Realization-based taxation is preserved. Valuation of unrealized assets is avoided. Ownership alone never triggers tax.

### 6. Why the frameworks remain independent by design

Each framework can be evaluated on its own merits. Each can be adopted or rejected independently. Combining them would increase complexity and invite avoidance. Separation is a feature, not a weakness.

### 7. How this architecture differs from wealth taxes and mark-to-market systems

Wealth taxes and mark-to-market systems attempt to solve time and scale simultaneously. They rely on continuous valuation and taxation. This architecture decomposes the problem and prices each dimension separately.

- It retains realization as the triggering event.
- The result is greater durability and lower administrative risk.

### 8. How this section should be read

This document does not propose simultaneous adoption. It explains why the tools fit together cleanly without depending on each other. They should be viewed as modular components, not a bundled mandate. The behavioral argument in subsection 4 describes why the framework functions more effectively when deferral is priced; it does not condition the framework's validity on any particular companion measure.

## Scope of Capital Income Covered

Long-term capital gains and qualified dividends are treated identically. All illustrative rate ceilings apply to realized LTCG and qualified dividends. Ordinary dividends and non-qualified investment income are outside the scope.
